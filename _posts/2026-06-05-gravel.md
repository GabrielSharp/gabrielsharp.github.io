## Bike's Purpose

I would say there are three categories of bikes on this list. When trying to find the (closest to) ideal *gravel* bike you need to know what you expect from the machine. Firstly the **ratio** of road, light gravel and what I will call tough gravel - which is MTB area really, that you expect to ride on. Some of the websites present a chart to a given model of a bike or component (for example 8bar bikes). I think that the higher the ratio of gravel to tarmac the more desired the suspension of some sort may be. Additionally if there is *tough gravel* in your focus, it may become a crucial point.

In the past 2 years there has been a trend of mounting wider and **wider tires** to our bikes - both road and gravel ones. In the latter it has become so obsessive that it is not uncommon to fit an MTB tire. What is even more hilarious, it has aero benefits when paired with rims of certain width. To be future proof and flexible in terms of choosing the terrain, the bias will be towards framesets that allow at least 45mm wide tires.  

And then come the **mounting points** on the frameset. If you are reading this there are two options: either you don't have a team to support you during a long racing event, or you plan to participate in events that don't even allow such support. At the same time you may want to enjoy backpacking of some sort. Considering these options as well as my own desires I will be strongly biased towards frames that have multiple mounting points and/or internal storage. 

All things combined the bikes will range from *light gravel racing* (0) to *tough gravel bikepacking* (100) focused and everything in between. The numbers next to those two categories is subjective range in which I will asign the purpose of the frameset. Meaning that the light aero, low stack (but still gravel) bike will get rating 0, and probably a steel framest with relaxed geometry, some suspension and multiple mounting points will get rating 100. It **does not** mean that 100 is better than 0, it is just a way to categorize the objects of this research.

Steel pros:
- steel is real and looks classy,
- more forgiving in terms of torque and weight you can carry,
- in case of emergency, welding is very popular around the world,
- smooth ride, damping vibrations,
- a lot of mounting points for racks, paneers etc,
- lot of small workshops and manufacturers in Europe.

Steel cons:
- modern steel framesets are approaching premium price range,
- no storage in downtube,
- can corrode if not looked after properly,
- slightly heavier than carbon (~ 1 kg).

Titanium pros:
- lighter than steel,
- scratches? What scratches?
- looks classy with colorful accessories,
- gaining in popularity, maybe prices will drop.

Titanium cons:
- racier than steel, but carbon is faster,
- small choice,
- usually pricy (looking at you [Passoni](https://passoni.com/en)),
- no downtube storage.

Internal routing is mostly viable with carbon framesets, although it's also becoming a standard option in modern steel and Ti frames. Wether it is advantage or disadvantage is, again, the matter of the bike purpose and your budget, and skills. More on that in the [Groupset & cockpit](#groupset-cockpit) section.

Summing up, *to me* it is a matter of answernig the honest question: do I want to compete and have capability to do so? If yes then go carbon, if not, go with titanium or steel, whichever looks better and makes you ride more. Performance-wise carbon also wins when the tarmac shows up on majority of your riding, espiecially when avoiding tough gravel. But in the end it is your decision and what you see here is mostly reasoning. Emotional factor is not considered here, although it may be the strongest motivation for riding a bike and should not be ignored. 

In all cases carbon fork is probably the best way to go and it will not be discussed further. Unless you want to attach 30 kg of stuff to it for some reason.

## Suspension

There are few ways to introdude suspension to a gravel bike:
1. Full suspension - like in Specialized Diverge STR,
2. Suspension fork - normal like Rock Shox Rudy or inverted like Cane Creek Invert CS,
3. Leaf fork suspension - Lauf suspension fork,
4. Stem suspension - Specialized Future Shock or Red Shift Shock Stop.

Full suspension is the most effective in damping and usually comes with MTB bikes. In case of Specialized Diverge STR, user can adjust the level of damping which makes it versitile, while also being sealed system with no maintenance needed. Opinions are rather good, but on the Specialized website it seems like it is no longer present in the latest models.

Suspension forks, while providing excellent damping come with two drawbacks. Firstly they need to be maintained which may require professional tools, compressed air and variety of lubricants. Moreover it needs to be serviced every few hundreds kilometers which makes it very bad considering that gravel activities like ultra races or backpacking can already approach this level of distance. Moreover they add around 1 kg with respect to non-suspension carbon fork. 

Leaf fork suspension is a product made by Lauf specifically for Seigla bike. While it may not be pretty for everyone it seems to be close to a perfect solution. It provides suspension appropriate for most gravel riding, while being a very lightweight option. What is more, it is zero-maintenance product which just sits on your fork and you can forget about it. The downsides are, firstly, its price and availability - although the company comes from Iceland, they manufacture in USA, which results in pricy shipping and additional texes. Secondly, there are no mounting racks on the fork. 

Specialized future shock is not only a stem suspension but a whole module that goes through a bike's headtube. It provides dampening of high frequency vibrations from light gravel, but is not as effective elsewhere. Also it needs to be serviced every 500 km which may not be ideal for everyone. Set and forget is luxury afterall. The red shift suspension stem is zero-maintenance option, but provides the smallest level of dampening of all aforementioned options.
